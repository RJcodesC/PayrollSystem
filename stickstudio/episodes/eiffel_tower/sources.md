# Sources & fact check — "The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower"

| Claim in the video | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1925, Victor Lustig "sold" the Eiffel Tower | Widely reported | Smithsonian Magazine, The Hustle, Amusing Planet, France Today |
| He posed as a government official and met scrap dealers at a fancy hotel | Widely reported | Hôtel de Crillon; posed as an official of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs |
| Reason given: tower costly to maintain / would be torn down | Widely reported | Newspaper reports about upkeep costs inspired it |
| He told dealers to keep it secret | Widely reported | Framed as avoiding public outcry |
| André Poisson was the buyer | Widely reported | |
| Lustig hinted at a bribe, which convinced Poisson he was a real official | Widely reported (anecdotal) | Classic part of the story |
| Fled to Austria; Poisson too embarrassed to report it | Widely reported | |
| He came back and tried again; this buyer went to police; Lustig escaped | Reported, details vary | Narrated as **"the story goes"**, and on-screen label says "(the story goes)" |
| Number of dealers (5 vs 6), exact payment | Disputed | Deliberately **not** stated in the video |

Dramatised elements (not presented as fact): dialogue lines, the newspaper design and headline,
character designs, the "AGAIN?!" signs, the police-station scene layout.
